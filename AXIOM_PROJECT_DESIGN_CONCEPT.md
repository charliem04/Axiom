# Axiom — Project Design Concept

> A daily cultural ritual designed to replace disposable scrolling with memorable discovery.

**Working concept — September 2026**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Axiom is a culture-forward discovery app built around one deliberate daily experience rather than an infinite recommendation feed.

Each day, the user receives a carefully presented cultural object—an album, film, poem, artwork, design movement, photograph, short article, architectural work, historical artifact, or similar experience—paired with the context that makes it memorable.

The product is intentionally designed against the dominant clip-by-clip attention model. Short content is welcome, but disposable content is not. A three-minute poem or a five-minute design exhibit can be valuable if it leaves the user with a new idea, artist, movement, talking point, or direction for further exploration.

The long-term platform grows outward from a single reusable primitive: the **Daily Entry**. Entries can later form cultural trails, weekly or monthly Taste Passports, recommendation chains passed between people, personal cultural maps, and curator-led collections without weakening the clarity of the core ritual.

The initial product should remain narrow. Its purpose is not to maximize time spent in-app. Its purpose is to become a trusted daily cultural institution in the user's existing app ecosystem: quick to open, visually memorable, intellectually dense, and easy to leave once the day's experience is complete.

---

## 2. Vision, North Star & Product Philosophy

### North Star

**Make the user's world meaningfully larger—one cultural experience at a time.**

### Primary Value Unit

The product should optimize for **something retained**, not **content consumed**.

A successful session is one where the user leaves with a new interest, new context, or a memorable idea—even if the session lasted only a few minutes.

### Core Product Principles

- **Scarcity over abundance:** One strong selection is more valuable than hundreds of disposable options.
- **Depth over velocity:** Context transforms an item from content into a cultural experience.
- **Curiosity over compulsion:** The retention loop should be “I wonder what tomorrow is,” not endless stimulation.
- **Expansion over reinforcement:** Personalization should help the user travel beyond their established tastes instead of trapping them inside a recommendation bubble.
- **Human taste over visible automation:** AI can support research and matching behind the scenes, while the product should feel curated, intentional, and culturally credible.
- **Completion over session time:** Opening, learning, saving a thought, and leaving is a successful session.
- **Short is not shallow:** Length is flexible; density, intentionality, and memorability matter more than duration.

## 3. Market Positioning & Marketability

Axiom is best positioned not as another recommendation engine, but as a **daily cultural ritual**: part museum, part magazine, part knowledgeable friend, part page-a-day book, and part record-store clerk.

Its differentiation comes from deliberate limitation and editorial trust.

### Marketable Product Promise

- One meaningful cultural experience each day
- No infinite home feed
- More context, less noise
- Discovery designed to expand taste rather than reinforce it
- A beautiful archive of what the user has encountered
- A product that respects the user's attention

### Potential Positioning Lines

- **One remarkable piece of culture every day.**
- **Discover more. Scroll less.**
- **You should leave with something.**
- **A daily cultural ritual for the modern attention span.**

## 4. Ideal User Persona

### Primary Persona: The Culturally Curious, Digitally Saturated User

**Age range:** Broadly 18–35 at launch, with potential to expand well beyond that range.

**Behavior:** Uses Spotify, YouTube, Instagram/TikTok, Letterboxd, Pinterest, newsletters, podcasts, or similar media discovery tools; saves large amounts of content but often does not return to it.

**Motivation:** Wants better taste, better recommendations, richer conversation, broader knowledge, and exposure to things outside their normal orbit without doing hours of research.

**Pain point:** Abundance has made discovery effortless but attachment difficult. They frequently consume without retaining much of what they saw.

**Desired feeling:** “I learned something cool today, and now I want to know more.”

### Secondary Personas

- **The Creative:** Designer, musician, filmmaker, writer, photographer, or student looking for fresh reference points and inspiration.
- **The Recovering Doomscroller:** Wants a lower-stimulation digital habit without abandoning entertainment or curiosity.
- **The Culture Enthusiast:** Already follows cinema, music, art, books, design, or history and values thoughtful curation.
- **The Social Explorer:** Likes having interesting things to share with friends and wants culture to feel transmitted between people rather than merely served by an algorithm.

## 5. Core Product Object: The Daily Entry

The **Daily Entry** is the atomic unit of the product.

Every current and future feature should either improve, organize, connect, transmit, or contextualize Entries.

### Recommended Entry Anatomy

1. **Hero / Reveal**  
   A strong image, album cover, still, object, quote, title, or visual identity that immediately establishes the day's selection.

2. **Why Today**  
   A concise editorial introduction that tells the user why this item is worth their attention.

3. **The Work**  
   The cultural object itself or a legal/approved route to experience it: embedded excerpt, linked album, trailer, poem, article, gallery, etc.

4. **The Artist / Maker**  
   Who made it, what stage of their life or career they were in, and why their perspective matters.

5. **The Moment**  
   Relevant historical, geographic, social, technological, or cultural context.

6. **Why It Mattered**  
   What changed because of the work, or what it represents within its field.

7. **Listen / Look / Notice For**  
   A short prompt that improves active engagement without making the experience feel academic.

8. **The Ripple**  
   Influences, descendants, related genres, movements, artists, locations, or ideas.

9. **Take This With You**  
   One concise idea or talking point designed to remain with the user after the app closes.

10. **Reflection**  
    Lightweight response: New artist, New idea, New genre, New historical connection, Want to explore further, or Didn't connect with me.

## 6. Content Categories

The category system should be broad enough to make Axiom feel culturally expansive, but launch with only enough categories to support consistently excellent curation.

Potential categories include:

- Music

- Visual Art
- Graphic Design
- Architecture
- Fashion

- Cultural History

- Places & Scenes

The content strategy should prioritize quality and memorability over category quantity.

## 7. App User Workflow

### 1. Daily Arrival

The user opens Axiom from a notification, habit, widget, or curiosity.

The home screen contains today's single Entry—not a feed.

### 2. Reveal

The day's cultural object is introduced with a strong visual/title and short editorial hook.

### 3. Experience

The user listens, watches, reads, observes, or explores the work.

Duration can range from a few minutes to an hour or more depending on the object.

### 4. Context Layers

The user progresses through optional but compelling layers: artist, historical context, genre or movement, influence, and “notice this” guidance.

### 5. Retention Moment

The Entry concludes with **Take This With You** and a lightweight reflection.

### 6. Archive

The completed Entry joins the user's personal library. The user can save or favorite it and return later.

### 7. Anticipation

Tomorrow's Entry is hinted at but unavailable.

The product creates curiosity rather than an invitation to keep scrolling.

### 8. Return

The next day, the cycle repeats.

Over time, the archive becomes a personal history of cultural exploration.

## 9. Signature Expansion: Taste Passport

Taste Passport should arrive only after the Daily Entry has proven itself.

It packages multiple Entries into a coherent cultural world rather than delivering isolated recommendations.

### Example

**Spend this week in 1990s Hong Kong cinema.**

The Passport could include:

- One film
- One album or musical context
- One clothing or design reference
- One photographer or visual artist
- One article or essay
- One food or place reference
- One historical explanation

The user's profile gradually becomes a passport of eras, cities, scenes, artistic movements, and subcultures explored.

This creates identity and progress without relying on conventional points, levels, or compulsive streak mechanics.

---

## 10. Signature Expansion: Human Transmission

Culture should eventually feel passed between people rather than merely distributed by software.

A user can pass an Entry they genuinely value to another person, and the product records its lineage.

**Charlie → Tyler → Emma → Diego → Sarah**

A user could discover that a film, album, poem, or artwork traveled through thousands of people across many cities or countries.

The story of transmission becomes part of the cultural object itself.

This is a social mechanic that reinforces meaning rather than volume.

## 11. Design Recommendations

### No Infinite Home Feed

The home screen should confidently present the current Entry.

Browsing belongs in the archive, collections, or intentionally requested exploration—not beneath today's content.

### Editorial Visual Hierarchy

Use magazine- and museum-inspired composition:

- Large imagery
- Confident typography
- Restrained motion
- Generous whitespace
- Strong pacing

### Progressive Disclosure

Do not present the Entry as a wall of text.

Reveal context in digestible layers that reward continued attention.

### Anticipation UI

After completion, subtly reveal that tomorrow's page exists without allowing it to be consumed early.

The physical page-a-day calendar metaphor is a useful design reference.

### Low-Stimulation Motion

Animations should support transitions, reveal, and focus—not compete for attention.

Avoid hyperactive UI patterns copied from short-form platforms.

### Completion State

Give the Entry a satisfying end.

The interface should clearly communicate that the user is done for today.

### Archive as Identity

Past Entries should form a beautiful personal record rather than a generic bookmarks screen.

### Cultural Credibility

Credits, sources, curator identity, dates, creators, and licensing should be visible and handled with care.

### Accessibility

Readable typography, scalable text, transcripts/captions, reduced-motion support, alt text, high contrast, and clear content duration should be first-class.

## 12. MVP Establishment

The MVP should test one question:

> **Will users repeatedly return for one thoughtfully curated cultural experience per day?**

### MVP Must Have

- Account or lightweight identity
- Simple onboarding with broad category interests
- Exactly one available Daily Entry
- Entry content model supporting text, images, external media links/embeds, metadata, sources, and duration
- Layered Entry sections
- Completion state
- Save/favorite
- Personal history/archive
- Reflection feedback
- Notification preference
- Editorial/admin workflow for scheduling Entries
- Basic product analytics

### Explicitly Out of Scope for MVP

- Taste Passport
- Recommendation chains
- Full cultural map
- Public social profiles
- Comments or follower graph
- Creator marketplace
- Complex AI chat interface
- Infinite discovery feed
- Heavy gamification
- Large-scale user-generated content

### Suggested MVP Content Inventory

Launch only when there is enough scheduled material to protect the ritual from operational pressure.

A useful target is **30–60 polished Entries prepared before public release**, with an editorial pipeline capable of maintaining a rolling buffer.

## 13. Project Requirements

### Functional Requirements

- Schedule and publish one global or segmented Entry per date
- Support multiple media/content types inside one consistent Entry model
- Store user completion, save, reflection, and category-preference signals
- Maintain user archive/history
- Send configurable daily notifications
- Provide editorial preview, draft, scheduling, source/credit, and revision capabilities
- Support deep links to individual Entries
- Provide basic analytics dashboards or event export

### Non-Functional Requirements

- Fast perceived load time and image/media optimization
- Reliable scheduled publishing across time zones
- Accessible mobile-first interface
- Strong moderation/editorial controls before any public contribution feature
- Secure authentication and minimal collection of personal data
- Source attribution and copyright/licensing discipline
- Scalable content schema so new categories and Passport collections do not require a rewrite
- Analytics designed around healthy product outcomes rather than raw time-on-app

---

## 14. Suggested Technical Direction

The system should be built around a flexible Entry model and a lightweight editorial workflow.

Exact technologies should remain open during concept validation.

The project should eventually support:

- A mobile-first client experience
- User identity and archive state
- A structured content store for Entries
- Editorial drafting, review, and scheduling
- Media links or approved embeds
- Source and attribution metadata
- Preference and reflection signals
- Relationships between artists, works, movements, eras, and places
- Basic analytics around completion and return behavior

Complex recommendation infrastructure is not necessary for the first version.

The core experience should work through strong curation before advanced personalization is introduced.

## 15. Editorial & Content Strategy

Content quality is not a support function for this product.

**Content quality is the product.**

### Selection Standard

An Entry should be:

- Culturally meaningful
- Interesting without requiring prior expertise
- Explainable in layers
- Capable of producing a memorable takeaway

### Mix

Balance recognizable anchors with adjacent discoveries and true wildcards.

Familiarity earns trust. Novelty expands taste.

### Voice

The editorial voice should be:

- Curious
- Informed
- Concise
- Non-academic
- Respectful of the original cultural context

### Research

Every historical or biographical claim should be sourceable.

Complex or disputed subjects should not be flattened into a neat story for engagement.

### Duration Labeling

Tell users upfront whether today's experience is approximately:

- 3 minutes
- 12 minutes
- 45 minutes
- A full film or album

### Rights

Build around metadata, commentary, transformative editorial framing, licensed assets, public-domain material, and approved embeds or links.

Do not assume a recommendation app has the right to redistribute the work itself.

---

## 16. Personalization & Novelty Model

Personalization should guide the user without becoming a mirror.

The app should know enough to build trust and enough to intentionally violate expectation.

A conceptual future mix could be:

- **~60% likely interest**
- **~25% adjacent exploration**
- **~15% deliberate wildcard**

This ratio should not be treated as a fixed algorithmic requirement.

It expresses the product philosophy:

> Relevance is useful, but complete relevance defeats the purpose.

A future **novelty distance** score could estimate how culturally far an Entry is from the user's established interests while preserving editorial quality.

---

## 17. Success Metrics

Avoid defining success primarily through total session duration.

Metrics should reflect ritual formation, completion, retention, and genuine cultural exploration.

### Core Metrics

- **Daily / weekly return rate:** Does the ritual create voluntary curiosity?
- **Entry completion rate:** Do users reach the end of the experience?
- **Save rate:** Which Entries create enough value to revisit?
- **Reflection signal:** How often do users report a new artist, genre, idea, historical connection, or desire to explore further?
- **Follow-on exploration:** Do users open the album, film, artist, article, or related material outside the app?
- **Breadth over time:** Does the user's archive become more diverse across categories, eras, countries, genres, and movements?
- **Organic sharing:** Do users pass Entries because they found them genuinely interesting?
- **Notification-to-open without fatigue:** Can Axiom sustain the daily ritual without becoming another nagging notification source?

## 18. Risks & Design Guardrails

### Content Quality Becomes Inconsistent

Maintain a strong editorial bar and launch buffer.

One weak Entry matters more when the product only gives one item per day.

### Scarcity Feels Restrictive Rather Than Special

Make the Entry rich enough to justify the limitation.

Maintain archives and optional follow-on links without exposing another endless feed.

### Personalization Recreates the Filter Bubble

Reserve explicit exploration capacity and build diversity into editorial planning.

### The App Becomes Educational Homework

Keep context conversational, layered, visually rich, and optional.

Curiosity should lead. Instruction should support.

### Gamification Undermines the Philosophy

Prefer passports, maps, archives, and meaningful milestones over points, leaderboards, and punitive streaks.

### AI Erodes Cultural Trust

Use AI as an internal tool with human review for research, tagging, connections, accessibility, and recommendation candidates.

Preserve visible authorship, sourcing, and editorial accountability.

### Copyright or Licensing Limits Media

Design Entries to remain valuable even when the canonical work is experienced through a licensed embed or external service.

### Daily Production Load Becomes Expensive

Build reusable templates, a content queue, contributor/editor workflows, and evergreen Entries that can be scheduled intentionally.

---

## 19. Monetization Direction

Monetization should not define the MVP.

The core experience should first prove daily value.

Any future monetization should preserve trust and avoid turning editorial selections into disguised advertising.

### Possible Directions

- **Free + premium depth:** Keep the daily ritual broadly accessible; premium could unlock deeper collections, advanced archives/maps, offline experiences, or extended Passports.
- **Curated editions:** Paid thematic journeys or collaborations with respected curators or institutions.
- **Institutional partnerships:** Museums, festivals, galleries, publishers, record stores, universities, or cultural organizations may sponsor clearly labeled editions without controlling ordinary editorial recommendations.
- **Membership:** A membership model may fit the cultural-institution identity better than aggressive ad inventory.

### Avoid

- Pay-to-rank recommendations
- Hidden sponsored Daily Entries
- Engagement-maximizing ad feeds
- Monetization that compromises curation

## 20. Validation Plan Before Full Production

Before committing to a full build:

1. Create **7–14 handcrafted Daily Entries** in a clickable prototype or lightweight web/mobile build.
2. Test whether users understand the promise without explanation: **one thing worth your attention every day**.
3. Observe whether users naturally complete Entries and whether layered context feels enriching or excessive.
4. Interview users after several days about what they actually remember—not merely whether they liked the design.
5. Test a mix of 3-minute, 10-minute, and 30–60-minute experiences.
6. Test recognizable vs adjacent vs wildcard selections.
7. Measure whether an unexpected recommendation reduces or increases trust.
8. Validate notification tone and frequency.
9. Only expand into personalization, Passports, chains, or social mechanics after the daily loop proves repeat value.

---

## 21. Key Product Decisions to Preserve

These decisions should act as guardrails throughout development:

- The Daily Entry remains the home-screen centerpiece.
- There is no infinite recommendation feed in the core experience.
- An Entry is a cultural experience plus context, not merely a link or recommendation card.
- Users should be able to finish for the day.
- Personalization expands taste rather than only maximizing predicted engagement.
- AI supports the product but does not need to be the product personality or visible curator.
- Taste Passport and human recommendation chains are extensions of the same Entry system, not separate apps.
- The archive should become a meaningful personal record of cultural exploration.
- Product health is measured by retention, completion, memory, exploration, and trust—not raw consumption.

## 22. Brand Territory

### Brand Goal

Axiom should feel like a modern cultural publication or institution, not a conventional content platform.

It should combine the intimacy of a knowledgeable friend with the authority of a thoughtful magazine and the curiosity of walking into a gallery or record store without knowing what you will find.

### Brand Characteristics

- Mysterious
- Editorial
- Intelligent
- Cultured
- Restrained
- Contemporary
- Human
- Curious
- Memorable
- Confident without being elitist

### Brand Promise

**You should leave with something.**

### Working Pitch

**One remarkable piece of culture every day, with the story and context that make it worth remembering.**

### Alternative Pitch

**A daily cultural ritual for people who want to discover more and scroll less.**

### Internal Product Statement

**A digital cultural institution designed for the attention span of the modern person.**

---

## 23. Closing Direction

Build the narrow product first.

If users begin to look forward to tomorrow's Entry, remember what they encountered, and follow discoveries into the wider world, the central thesis is working.

Everything else—Taste Passports, cultural maps, recommendation chains, guest curators, partnerships, and personalization—can then deepen a behavior that already has meaning.

The product should never lose sight of the original inversion:

> Most platforms say: “Here are 100 things. Maybe one will matter.”

> Axiom says: **“Here is one thing. We believe it is worth your attention.”**
