# Axiom Editorial Idea Bank

> A categorized inspiration library for Axiom's Daily Entry system.

## Purpose

This document is a long-term reservoir of possible Axiom editorials: works, objects, artists, scenes, places, ideas, events, technologies, and cultural oddities that can become Daily Entries or larger Taste Passports.

## Legend

- **[ORIGINAL]** — explicitly introduced in the earlier Axiom brainstorming in this chat.
- Unmarked items — expanded ideas added to broaden the editorial bank.

## Editorial Standard

Axiom should not simply become a list of culturally approved “great works.” Every Entry should have an editorial thesis.

Before developing an idea, ask:

- Why is this worth someone's attention today?
- What larger cultural story does it open?
- What should the user actively notice?
- What influenced it?
- What did it influence?
- What can the user retain or tell someone afterward?
- Is it an Anchor, Adjacent discovery, or Wildcard?
- Can it connect to another Entry or future Taste Passport?

## Suggested Mix

- **Anchor:** recognizable enough to build trust.
- **Adjacent:** connected to likely interests but broadens taste.
- **Wildcard:** something the user probably would never have searched for.
- **Microculture:** niche scene, local phenomenon, internet community, or subculture.
- **Object Story:** hidden cultural history inside an everyday object.
- **Connection:** a lineage linking seemingly unrelated cultural artifacts.
- **Place:** a city, venue, street, building, or neighborhood as cultural catalyst.
- **Moment:** a concentrated period of historical change.
- **Person:** a creator, thinker, organizer, designer, engineer, or cultural transmitter.
- **Idea:** a concept that changes how someone interprets culture.

# Editorial Template for Converting an Idea Into an Entry

For every candidate, define:

- **Object:** What exactly is today's thing?
- **Hook:** Why should someone stop and care?
- **Creator:** Who made it?
- **Moment:** What historical/cultural environment produced it?
- **Experience:** What should the user watch, hear, read, inspect, or do?
- **Notice:** What detail should the user pay attention to?
- **Meaning:** Why did it matter?
- **Ripple:** What did it influence?
- **Connection:** What unexpected lineage makes it memorable?
- **Take This With You:** One concise takeaway worth remembering.
- **Time:** Approximate experience duration.
- **Distance:** Anchor / Adjacent / Wildcard.
- **Next Path:** What future Entry or Passport could naturally follow?
- **Sources:** Is there enough credible material to research the Entry responsibly?

# Editorial Guardrails

- Do not choose something only because it is famous.
- Do not choose obscurity merely to appear sophisticated.
- Avoid turning Axiom into cultural homework.
- Do not flatten complex historical or political subjects into trivia.
- Do not treat cultures as aesthetic moodboards without context.
- Intentionally diversify geography, language, gender, era, class, and cultural origin.
- Mix institutions and canonical art with vernacular, folk, street, commercial, and internet culture.
- Treat everyday objects seriously when their histories justify it.
- Short Entries can be profound.
- Long works should explain why the time investment is worthwhile.
- Every Entry needs a reason to exist beyond “this is good.”
- The user should leave able to tell someone else at least one interesting thing.

# Core Axiom Editorial Test

> **Could someone tell a friend something interesting about this tonight?**

If yes, it is probably Axiom material.
